# Highlighting Rules (context-aware bold)

The goal: in a drafted email, make the few details that matter most to the recipient stand
out, so they can scan the message and immediately see what to act on, confirm, or price.
Bold is the tool. Use it with discipline. If everything is bold, nothing is.

This applies to the rendered HTML draft, where bold shows. Bold the substance, never the
pleasantries.

## How to apply

1. Read the email and decide its context and intent: what is this email trying to get done?
2. From the map below, bold the detail types that are decision-critical in that context.
3. Bold the specific value, or the few words that carry the meaning, not whole sentences.
4. Keep it sparing: a handful of bolds per email at most. Bold the things the recipient must
   answer, confirm, or that change the decision or the price.

## Context to highlight map

**Asking for or giving a freight quote (carriers and forwarders).** Bold the money and rates
(ocean freight, origin and destination charges, surcharges, the war risk surcharge, currency
amounts), the cold-chain essentials (genset power, temperature monitoring, liability for a
temperature excursion), and the routing question (the via-port or land-bridge point). These
are what make a quote comparable and trustworthy.

**Pricing or finance (quoting to a buyer, costs, payment).** Bold the price or rate figures
and their currency, payment or Incoterms, the margin or discount if stated, and any quote
validity or expiry date. Clarity on money first.

**Chronology and timing.** Bold dates, deadlines, transit times, lead times, durations,
validity or expiry dates, and cut-off times. Anything time-bound the recipient must hit or
plan around.

**Quantity, size, volume, mass.** Bold weights (kg, tonnes), container sizes (20ft, 40ft
reefer), dimensions, pallet or carton counts, volumes (CBM), quantities, and minimum order
quantities. The metrics that size the job.

**Cargo value and insurance.** Bold the declared cargo value figures (for example AUD 50,000
and AUD 100,000) and the insurance type (marine, road).

**Compliance, licensing, setup (UAE firms and authorities).** Bold the licence or permit
type, the importer or customs code, the ownership percentage, the specific mandatory
requirement or approval body, and any cost, fee, or timeframe.

**Binding or legal terms.** Bold the committed figure, the term length, exclusivity, fees or
penalties, and the deadline. Note that binding or legal matters usually escalate rather than
get a drafted reply (see `thread-context.md`); when a reply is drafted, these terms are what
to surface.

**Action or confirmation requested.** Bold the specific action or yes/no confirmation being
asked for, the named owner if relevant, and any reference, booking, or account number.

## Never bold

Greetings, thank-yous, filler, hedging, or whole paragraphs. Do not bold more than the few
genuinely decision-critical items. The signature is bold by its own rule, not by this one.

## Worked example (freight quote reply)

Context: replying to a carrier's indicative rates, driving toward a pricing-ready quote.
Bolded items: the routing question (**by road or bonded land bridge**), the cold-chain ask
(**continuous reefer temperature control, including genset power, temperature monitoring, and
liability for any temperature excursion**), and the **war risk surcharge**. Everything else
stays plain. Three bolds, each the crux of a point.
